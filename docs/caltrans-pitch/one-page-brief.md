# Environmental Sentinel Network for Caltrans District 4

## Verified Corridor Intelligence for Cleaner, Safer, Healthier Rights-of-Way

Prepared by Pure Street / Environmental Sentinel Network (ESN)

Status: Draft for agency briefing and review

## The Problem
Public corridors often show environmental decline before formal systems can fully interpret the pattern. Litter, illegal dumping, stormwater-adjacent debris, sanitation hazards, access obstructions, abandoned materials, graffiti, overgrowth, and unsafe debris can recur across frontage roads, ramps, underpasses, sidewalks, and jurisdictional boundaries.

Traditional reporting systems can capture individual complaints, but agencies and partners also need verified status, severity, recurrence, location context, response history, and evidence of durable resolution.

## The ESN Solution
The Environmental Sentinel Network is a field intelligence and corridor stewardship platform that helps agencies and partners:

- identify recurring environmental hazards;
- verify field conditions;
- prioritize locations by severity and recurrence;
- coordinate cleanup or mitigation with partners;
- document before-and-after outcomes;
- generate corridor health metrics and public impact summaries.

ESN is not a replacement for existing agency systems. It is a supplemental verification, coordination, and reporting layer.

## How It Works

1. Observe: field condition is reported with location, category, notes, and evidence.
2. Verify: trained reviewers confirm whether the condition is active, changed, resolved, recurring, or misclassified.
3. Prioritize: issues are ranked by severity, recurrence, access impact, drainage proximity, and unresolved age.
4. Coordinate: agency and partner teams receive structured evidence packets.
5. Resolve: cleanup or mitigation occurs.
6. Prove: before-and-after documentation supports evaluation and public reporting.

## Proposed Pilot
Pure Street proposes a limited Caltrans District 4 corridor intelligence pilot in Oakland / Alameda County, with final geography selected collaboratively by Caltrans.

Potential pilot areas may include:

- I-880 / East Oakland corridor conditions;
- San Pablo Avenue / West Oakland-Emeryville corridor;
- International Boulevard corridor;
- freeway underpasses and frontage roads;
- Caltrans-selected recurring hotspot zones.

Recommended duration: 90 to 180 days.
