# ESN Agency Outreach Plan

Prepared for Pure Street / Environmental Sentinel Network (ESN)

## Purpose
This document defines the pathway for getting ESN in front of Caltrans and related public actors without overexposing proprietary methods or asking for too much too early.

## Primary Target
Caltrans District 4

Relevant internal audiences:

- Maintenance
- Operations
- Clean California
- Stormwater
- Public Affairs
- Environmental Planning
- Transportation Planning and Local Assistance
- Civil Rights / Title VI
- Adopt-A-Highway coordination
- Customer Service Request / public issue reporting staff

## Secondary Targets

- City of Oakland Public Works
- Oakland Department of Transportation
- Alameda County Transportation Commission
- Metropolitan Transportation Commission
- Alameda County Public Works
- Business Improvement Districts
- Corridor merchant associations
- community cleanup organizations
- environmental justice organizations
- university research partners
- philanthropic funders focused on civic tech, public health, climate, and infrastructure

## Outreach Doctrine
Do not ask first for a large contract. Ask for a briefing, technical review, and pilot scoping conversation.

First ask:

1. 30-minute briefing
2. identification of correct program contact
3. technical review
4. pilot corridor selection discussion
5. pathway toward MOU, sponsorship, grant partnership, or procurement pilot

## Meeting Opening Script
Thank you for meeting with me. I am Eric-Michael Wilson, founder of Pure Street and developer of the Environmental Sentinel Network. ESN is a field intelligence and corridor stewardship platform designed to help agencies and community partners identify, verify, prioritize, and resolve recurring environmental hazards, including litter, illegal dumping, stormwater-adjacent pollutants, sanitation hazards, access obstructions, abandoned materials, and safety hazards along public rights-of-way.

I am not here to suggest that ESN replaces existing Caltrans systems. I am here to show how it can strengthen existing work by adding verified field evidence, hotspot analysis, partner coordination, and measurable public accountability.

## Closing Script
I am not asking Caltrans to replace any system or make a large commitment today. I am asking for a structured next step: a District 4 technical review of ESN as a limited corridor intelligence pilot.

The pilot would test whether verified field reporting, recurrence detection, corridor health scoring, and public impact reporting can help Caltrans and its partners respond more effectively to litter, dumping, stormwater risk, access obstruction, and public-right-of-way hazards.

If the concept is useful, we can define one corridor, one evaluation period, one data governance framework, and one clear set of metrics.

- source code
- proprietary scoring formulas
- full backend architecture
- internal pricing floor
- vendor implementation plan
- private roadmaps
- non-public data models

- one-page brief
- short deck
- public landing page
- high-level pilot proposal
- data-governance summary
- sample dashboard screenshots
- implementation roadmap
