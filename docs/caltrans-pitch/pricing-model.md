# ESN Pricing Model for Agency Pilots

Prepared for Pure Street / Environmental Sentinel Network (ESN)

## Pricing Philosophy
ESN should be priced as a civic intelligence and corridor operations platform, not as a simple website, cleanup app, or reporting form.

The value to agencies comes from:

- verified field intelligence
- reduced duplicate reporting
- better prioritization
- improved cleanup coordination
- impact documentation
- public accountability
- grant and program reporting support
- corridor-level analytics
- stronger partner coordination

## Pricing Caution
Do not lead with the lowest price. Public agencies often expect pilots, scopes, deliverables, insurance, reporting, compliance review, staff time, and meetings. Underpricing can make ESN look unserious and can create unsustainable backend obligations.

## Recommended Pricing Tiers

### Tier 1: Lean Pilot
Estimated range: $25,000 to $50,000

Best for: proof of concept, limited corridor, early agency review.

Includes:

- One pilot corridor or defined hotspot zone
- Basic public landing page
- Basic reporting form
- Manual verification protocol
- Simple dashboard or spreadsheet-backed dashboard
- Hazard taxonomy
- Monthly summary report
- Final pilot memo
- Limited stakeholder meetings

Excludes unless added:

- Large field team
- Custom integrations
- advanced analytics
- multi-agency routing
- live API integration
- extensive media production
- full legal/compliance support

### Tier 2: Standard Pilot
Estimated range: $75,000 to $150,000

Best for: serious District 4 or city corridor demonstration.

Includes:

- One to three corridors
- Configured dashboard
- Verification queue
- Evidence packet generator
- Monthly impact reports
- Data governance framework
- Partner onboarding
- Field verification stipend budget
- Agency review meetings
- Public-facing pilot page
- Final evaluation report

Potential add-ons:

- Video walkthrough
- custom forms
- multilingual reporting
- Adopt-A-Highway sponsor dashboards
- stormwater-focused reporting module
- accessibility-focused reporting module

### Tier 3: Full Demonstration
Estimated range: $250,000 to $500,000

Best for: multi-corridor, multi-partner agency demonstration.

Includes:

- Multi-corridor deployment
- Dedicated dashboard
- Partner portal
- Field verification team
- Public accountability dashboard
- Advanced recurrence analysis
- evaluation partner support
- structured training program
- media and public communications package
- monthly and final evaluation reports
- expansion roadmap

### Tier 4: Annual Platform + Services Contract
Estimated range: $150,000 to $750,000+ annually depending on scope

Best for: recurring agency use after pilot.

Possible pricing structure:

- Platform license
- Monthly support retainer
- Field verification services
- Data/reporting services
- Partner coordination services
- Training and onboarding
- Custom integrations
- Evaluation reporting

## Pricing Floor
Do not accept an agency pilot below $25,000 unless:

- it is a non-cash strategic partnership with major credibility value;
- the scope is extremely limited;
- the agency provides staff, data, field support, and promotion;
- Pure Street retains all IP;
- there is a clear pathway to paid expansion.

## Negotiation Strategy
Start with outcome-based value, then scope.

Do not say:

- `I can build this cheap.`
- `I just need a chance.`
- `This is only an app.`

Say:

- `The pilot cost depends on corridor scope, verification intensity, reporting cadence, and integration requirements.`
- `We can start with a lean demonstration and scale only if the metrics justify it.`
- `The goal is not software alone. The goal is verified corridor intelligence and measurable improvement.`

## Agency-Safe Budget Language
Use this in public-facing materials:

`Pricing is presented as planning-level estimates for discussion. Final costs depend on pilot geography, duration, data requirements, reporting cadence, field verification model, integration requirements, insurance, legal review, and agency procurement process.`

## Recommended First Ask
Ask for:

1. 30-minute briefing.
2. Technical review.
3. Corridor selection discussion.
4. Permission to prepare a scoped pilot memo.
5. A path toward MOU, grant partnership, sponsorship, or procurement pilot.

Do not ask for a large contract in the first message.
